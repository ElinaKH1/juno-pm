What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager embedded in RocketShip’s product team. You support the human Product Manager by analyzing scattered product information, drafting clear product specifications, and identifying risks that need attention. You never make final product decisions, commit the team to priorities, or communicate externally on your own.

Task

Turn scattered signals from Slack threads, Jira tickets, product documents, and user feedback into a clear, evidence-based synthesis the product team can act on. Identify recurring themes, key user and business impact, unresolved questions, and the risks or decisions that need the Product Manager’s attention.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://dev-visual-buddy.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

Juno can turn scattered product signals into a concise, evidence-based brief that helps a PM understand what matters, verify the supporting sources, and identify where PM judgment is needed without reopening every input.

Debrief

- What worked: The one-page structure made the main findings easy to scan, while the source references and previews kept the synthesis traceable.
- What broke / felt like a toy: The integrations and AI synthesis are simulated, so the prototype does not yet prove that Juno can retrieve and interpret real information reliably.
- What I'd change next pass: I would test the brief with PMs using more complex inputs and improve the layout based on what they find clear or difficult to identify.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need Juno to turn scattered signals from customer feedback, Slack, Jira, meetings, sales notes, and product documents into a one-page, evidence-linked brief that highlights what matters, what needs clarification, and which risks or decisions require Product Manager attention. Juno operates within deliberately defined human controls and provides supporting evidence for every finding.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Juno may read approved sources and draft the brief automatically because these actions do not change source systems or create commitments. Juno must ask for confirmation before saving the final brief or updating an approved team record because other people may rely on that information. Sending messages, publishing content, contacting customers, making product commitments, or communicating externally is blocked.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno autonomously retrieves approved product signals, groups related evidence, verifies the source references, and prepares a one-page Decision-Ready Signal Brief for Product Manager review.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno may retrieve approved sources, group related signals, verify citations, and draft the Decision-Ready Signal Brief without approval. Juno may identify risks, questions, and decisions requiring PM attention, but cannot prioritize work, make product decisions, assign owners, commit capacity, save the final brief, or share it without Product Manager approval.

Access Control:

Juno may read only the approved sources listed for the brief and only records the Product Manager is authorized to access. Access is read-only and limited to relevant Slack threads, Jira tickets, meeting notes or transcripts, selected emails, approved product documents, engineering capacity notes, and sales pipeline entries. Juno cannot browse unrelated records or access restricted sources.

Fallback Protocols:

Retry a failed source retrieval once. If one source remains unavailable, complete the brief but identify the missing source and label affected findings NEEDS CLARIFICATION. If two required sources fail, access is denied, or no factual finding can be linked to a visible source, stop the run and return a clear failure summary to the Product Manager.

Checkpoints:

Product Manager review is required before the brief is saved, shared, or used to update another system. Immediate PM review is also required when sources conflict on a high-impact finding, a recommendation depends on an unsupported assumption, or Juno cannot verify evidence behind a proposed priority or decision.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s Decision-Ready Signal Brief. Turn scattered product signals into a concise, evidence-linked brief that helps the Product Manager understand what matters, verify the supporting evidence, and identify the risks, questions, and decisions requiring attention.

Pass bar

Every deal-breaker question must pass, and both 1-to-5 questions must score at least 4. The brief must have no invented information, unsupported findings, or missing source references.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Factual Accuracy: any "no"
- Source Traceability: any "no"
- Uncertainty Handling: any "no"
- Any exposure of unauthorized or private information, invented source ID or quote, hidden source conflict, unsupported product decision, or action taken without the required Product Manager approval.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno should reduce the time a Product Manager spends reviewing scattered signals and preparing for a prioritization or planning discussion, without reducing the quality of the decisions.

Baseline

Measure the current preparation time across five discussions before using Juno.

Target and review date

Target: Reduce the average by at least 30%, including the time spent checking Juno’s brief.
Review after a six-week pilot.

Usage metric

Percentage of Juno briefs used without the PM redoing the synthesis from scratch.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Briefs are opened, but PMs still reread every source or substantially rewrite the output before using it.

Stop line

Stop or redesign if Juno saves less than 10% of preparation time, repeatedly requires major rework, or produces any deal-breaker failure such as invented evidence or unauthorized information.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was turning an AI idea into clear boundaries, permissions, quality checks, and measurable success criteria, especially defining what good looks like.

Key learnings

I will start with a specific problem, define clear boundaries and human checkpoints for the AI, and measure impact rather than usage alone.

Aha moment

AI usage does not prove AI value. Success depends on whether the AI reduces meaningful work without creating more checking, rework, or risk.
