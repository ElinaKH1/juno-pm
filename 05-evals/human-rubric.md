# Human Evaluation Rubric · Juno

## What graders score

- **What you’re scoring:** Juno’s Decision-Ready Signal Brief
- **Value proposition:** Turn scattered product signals into a concise, evidence-linked brief that helps the Product Manager understand what matters, verify the supporting evidence, and identify the risks, questions, and decisions requiring attention.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Scale | 1 or No | 3 | 5 or Yes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Factual Accuracy (deal-breaker) | Does every factual finding accurately reflect the source material? | Yes / No | At least one factual finding misrepresents a source or includes invented or unsupported information. | n/a | Every factual finding is supported by the cited source, with no invented facts, numbers, quotes, users, or conclusions. |
| Source Traceability (deal-breaker) | Can every factual finding be traced to an existing source that actually supports it? | Yes / No | A source is missing, invented, inaccessible without explanation, incorrectly linked, or does not support the finding. | n/a | Every factual finding includes the correct source type and ID, and the cited source can be opened and verified. |
| Quality of Synthesis | Does the brief identify the most important recurring signals without simply repeating or listing the inputs? | 1 to 5 | The brief mostly repeats individual inputs, misses important themes, or gives equal weight to strong and weak signals. | The main themes are identified, but some findings are repetitive, weakly grouped, or not clearly connected to user or business impact. | The brief clearly groups related signals, highlights the strongest findings, explains why they matter, and preserves important differences or conflicts between sources. |
| Uncertainty Handling (deal-breaker) | Does the brief clearly distinguish findings from assumptions, single-source signals, conflicting evidence, and missing information? | Yes / No | An assumption, weak signal, or unresolved conflict is presented as a confirmed finding, or missing information is filled in without evidence. | n/a | Assumptions and single-source signals are visibly labelled, conflicts are surfaced, and missing information is marked NEEDS CLARIFICATION instead of being guessed. |
| PM Usefulness | Can a Product Manager understand what matters and what requires attention within two minutes? | 1 to 5 | The brief is difficult to scan, includes unnecessary detail, or leaves the Product Manager unclear about what needs attention. | The main findings are understandable, but the risks, decisions, questions, or next steps need clarification or reordering. | The brief is concise and easy to scan, clearly identifies what matters, and shows the decisions, risks, questions, and next steps requiring Product Manager attention. |

## Calibration

- **How many answers you’ll score, and how often:** Review 10 briefs each week: a random sample of 5, plus 5 briefs with low confidence, conflicting evidence, failed retrievals, or NEEDS CLARIFICATION labels.
- **Who scores them with you:** The Product Manager scores the weekly sample, with a second Product Manager reviewing 5 briefs once a month to check scoring consistency.
- **What happens when two of you disagree:** If scores differ by 2 or more points, or reviewers disagree on a deal-breaker, they review the sources together and agree on the final score. If the rubric caused the disagreement, update the scoring description and use the case as a calibration example.

## Pass bar

Every deal-breaker question must pass, and both 1-to-5 questions must score at least 4. The brief must have no invented information, unsupported findings, or missing source references.

**Fails outright, whatever the average:**

- Factual Accuracy: any "no"
- Source Traceability: any "no"
- Uncertainty Handling: any "no"
- Any exposure of unauthorized or private information, invented source ID or quote, hidden source conflict, unsupported product decision, or action taken without the required Product Manager approval.
